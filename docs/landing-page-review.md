# Repeti landing page review

Research date: 10 October 2026. Scope: the current public landing page and vocabulary-learning competitors. These are recommendations; the application has not been changed as part of this review.

## Main finding

The current page has a calm visual style and a working reveal-card sample. Its main opportunity is to explain the actual product more clearly: European Portuguese vocabulary, three exercise formats, English or Ukrainian translations, and progress saved separately for each account.

The headline, “Learn words every day,” could describe almost any vocabulary app. Most of the supporting copy repeats the idea of a daily habit. The page still demonstrates only the original reveal-card exercise, even though translation choices and matching pairs are now available.

European Portuguese is a useful focus, but it is not an exclusive feature: Drops and Practice Portuguese already serve this audience. Repeti should show its particular learning experience rather than claim that other apps only teach Brazilian Portuguese.

## Competitors

The descriptions below come from official product and help pages. Language examples describe the competitors, not languages currently taught by Repeti. Quizlet's examples were checked through indexed official documentation because direct page requests were blocked; this review does not claim to have inspected its live homepage visually.

| Competitor | Languages and vocabulary approach | Useful lesson for Repeti |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Drops](https://languagedrops.com/language/learn-european-portuguese) | Multiple languages, including European and Brazilian Portuguese. Its European Portuguese page presents visual vocabulary games and short practice sessions. | Show a touch-friendly exercise and make the session size easy to understand. |
| [Lingvist](https://lingvist.com/) | Multiple languages, including French, German and Brazilian Portuguese. Learners practise vocabulary in sentences, with adaptive repetition. | Explain how practice responds to previous results, and show the actual learning interface. |
| [Clozemaster](https://www.clozemaster.com/) | Many languages and language pairings. Learners complete missing words in sentences by choosing or typing an answer. | Put a concrete exercise example near the headline and explain the learning loop in plain language. |
| [Memrise](https://www.memrise.com/) | Multiple languages, including Spanish, French, German and Japanese. Its current product emphasizes native-speaker videos, vocabulary recall and spoken practice. | Demonstrate the experience early; give each step a clear learner action. |
| [Anki](https://apps.ankiweb.net/) | A general flashcard tool using custom or shared decks, suitable for many languages. Recall ratings drive scheduled reviews. | Explain repetition clearly and use real card and progress examples. |
| [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/content/quizlet101) | A general study tool using vocabulary or other study sets. Flashcards, Learn, Test and Match provide different ways to practise the same content. | Make the available exercise formats visible instead of hiding them behind a generic practice button. |
| [WordUp](https://www.zann.app/apps-page/wordup) | English vocabulary specifically. It presents useful words, examples and repeated practice. | A focused language product can explain which everyday needs its vocabulary serves. |
| [Practice Portuguese](https://www.practiceportuguese.com/) | European Portuguese specifically. A broader course product with listening material, vocabulary and personalized Smart Review. | State the Portuguese variety clearly and support the promise with actual product examples. |

Drops and Practice Portuguese are the closest comparisons for the language audience. Quizlet and Anki are useful comparisons for exercise variety and review mechanics; they are general study tools rather than equivalent curated language courses. Memrise and Practice Portuguese also offer broader learning experiences, so their full feature lists should not become a checklist for Repeti.

Practice Portuguese's [Smart Review documentation](https://help.practiceportuguese.com/article/52-how-does-smart-review-work) explains scheduled repetition and different practice modes. That is a possible future product direction, rather than a description of Repeti's current review system.

## What to keep

- The blue-and-white palette, readable typography and simple card layout.
- The explicit European Portuguese focus.
- The opportunity to interact with a word before registering.
- The short page and straightforward account creation flow.

## Recommended page structure

### 1. Specific hero and a useful demo

Suggested copy:

> **Build your everyday European Portuguese vocabulary.**
>
> Practise with reveal cards, translation choices and matching pairs. Use English or Ukrainian translations and save your progress to your account.

Proposed primary action: **Try a demo**. Secondary action: **Create an account**. Keep a visible **Log in** link for returning learners.

Expand the current one-word reveal into a short anonymous sample. Offer the three exercise formats using the same icons as the practice picker. A learner should be able to answer, see feedback and move to the next item. After completing the sample, offer account creation to save future progress.

This is a proposed feature: the current public demo only reveals one translation. Demo results should be distinguished from account progress. Any progress indicator should follow completed demo items; the existing fixed decorative bar does not show actual completion.

Replace repeated floating habit messages with concrete facts, such as **European Portuguese**, **English / Ukrainian translations** and **10, 30 or 100 words per session**. Avoid filling the hero with all of these at once; prioritize readability on a phone.

### 2. Show the three ways to practise

Use three compact cards, with the existing exercise icons and a small example:

| Exercise | Learner-facing explanation |
| --- | --- |
| Recall cards — YES / NO | Try to remember the translation. Reveal it if needed, then record whether you knew it. |
| Choose a translation | Pick the correct translation from four answers and receive feedback. |
| Match pairs | Connect Portuguese words with their translations using two taps. |

Use familiar vocabulary such as `janela` and `porta`. The mobile matching example should fit the product's four-pair layout. Do not imply that hints or skipping are available in every exercise.

### 3. Explain the session loop

Replace abstract habit descriptions with the actual steps:

1. Choose 10, 30 or 100 words and an exercise.
2. Practise and receive feedback.
3. See your results and return for a mix of new words and review.

Explain that previous results influence what needs more practice. Repeti currently uses answer history to mix new and review words; it does not schedule reviews at calendar-based spaced-repetition intervals.

### 4. Show what saved progress looks like

Include an actual dashboard or session-results preview with representative sample data, clearly labeled as a preview. Show the information the product already provides, rather than inventing scores, streaks or learning outcomes.

Explain the concrete account benefit: progress belongs to the signed-in learner. Correct answers in different exercise formats currently contribute to the same learning progress. Avoid presenting recognition in multiple-choice or matching as proof of speaking fluency.

### 5. Answer practical questions and close with one action

Suggested FAQ topics:

- **Which Portuguese?** European Portuguese, as used in Portugal.
- **Which translations?** English and Ukrainian. These are translation options, not additional target languages.
- **Do I need an account?** The public sample can be tried without signing in; registration is needed for personal saved practice progress.
- **Can I practise on my phone?** Yes, in the browser. Do not advertise an installable native app.
- **How do I choose a session?** Select a word count and one of the three exercises before starting.

Localize the landing page into English and Ukrainian using the same language controls as the application. The current landing copy is hardcoded in English.

End with **Create an account** and a short statement about saved progress. Publish pricing claims, testimonials or usage statistics only when there is verified information to support them.

## Priorities

| Priority | Change | Intended benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | Specific headline, concrete supporting copy and visible exercise choices | Visitors understand the language and activity immediately. |
| First | A short working demo with feedback | Visitors experience the product before deciding to register. |
| Next | Real progress preview and practical FAQ | Visitors understand the account benefit and basic product scope. |
| Next | Landing localization and mobile layout verification | The public page matches the languages and devices supported inside the app. |
| Later | Additional target languages, audio or scheduled spaced repetition | These require product work before they can appear as current landing-page features. |

The first iteration should focus on clearer copy and the exercise demo. Keep the existing visual direction; replacing the palette or introducing a large animated hero is less useful than showing how practice works.

## How to evaluate the changes

These are design hypotheses, not demonstrated conversion improvements. Compare demo starts, demo completions, registration completions and first completed account sessions, segmented by mobile and desktop where possible. Check that visitors can identify the target language, name the exercise choices and explain why they would create an account.

Keep the demo usable by keyboard, provide text labels alongside icons, and ensure answer feedback does not rely on color alone.

## Implementation

The landing recommendations were implemented after this review. The chosen headline is **Learn new words. Repeat daily. Speak with confidence.** The page includes an anonymous sample of all three exercise formats, exercise explanations, the session flow, the existing progress components with labeled sample data, six FAQs and English/Ukrainian localization. Public account pages also expose the shared language preference.

Demo answers stay in the current page and are not transferred to an account. Additional target languages, audio and scheduled spaced repetition remain future product ideas.

Verification covered the production build, the existing 43 tests, completion and feedback in all three demos, hint and matching-mistake tracking, language switching, keyboard interaction, and narrow layouts. Reusing the chart also exposed a React server-rendering mismatch in its SVG titles; using a single text child fixes it.
